Anomaly Detection
in Medical Imaging

Three tensions and the road ahead
How medical anomaly detection works today, the problems it still can't solve, and where the field is heading.
Brain MRIChest CTHistopathologyRetinal OCTChest X-ray
Presenter: Jin Er  ·  2026
Motivation · why this matters

You can't label every disease

  • Disease is rare, diverse, and long-tailed — most cases are normal
  • You can't enumerate every possible abnormality to label it
  • Anatomy + heterogeneity + clinical safety make supervised models brittle
  • → The one idea: learn "normal", flag the deviation

The same learn-normal, flag-deviation idea spans 7 datasets — chest X-ray, brain MRI, retina, dermoscopy, pathology.

Setup

How the task is framed

  • Train on normal only → build a "normal prior"; anomaly score = deviation from it
  • Granularity: image-level detection vs pixel-level localization / segmentation
  • Metrics: AUROC · AP · Dice / IoU — the metric choice turns out to matter

Why it matters · in the clinic

One "normal" model, four clinical jobs

Screening & triage

Surface the few abnormal scans so clinicians read them first — where per-disease labels can't scale.

Localization without labels

A lesion segmentation with zero annotations — the deviation map is already pixel-level.

OOD / quality control

Reject corrupted / out-of-distribution scans — a gatekeeper before any downstream model.

Rare & novel disease

Catch what was never in the training set — AD needs no positive examples.

The spine of this talk

Three tensions the field is resolving

① Represent "normal"

Rebuild the scan and read the residual — or embed it and measure feature distance.

input → reconstruction → anomaly map
e.g. on brain MRI, the residual highlights a tumour a normal-trained model can't reconstruct

② Fidelity vs sensitivity

In reconstruction methods, the noise paradox: erase the lesion without distorting healthy anatomy.

too much noise → false positives
e.g. over-noising also alters healthy anatomy → the pseudo-healthy scan drifts → false positives

③ Scarcity & generalization

Almost no abnormal data, and it must work across many modalities.

one model → MRI · CT · OCT
e.g. MVFA-AD adapts one CLIP backbone to brain MRI, liver CT and retinal OCT
Existing methods = answers to these tensions · the unresolved parts = future work

Reconstruct-and-compare: AE → GAN

Tension ②

The specificity bar

False positives decide deployment — with the noise paradox behind them

Tension ② · the real bar

Why specificity decides deployment

  • A balanced AUROC of 0.95 ≠ usable
  • At 1% prevalence: a few % false positives × many normals → alarms drown the clinician
  • → alert fatigue → abandoned; and "detect anything abnormal" is hard to clear (regulation)
  • ⇒ almost no unsupervised AD is in clinical use — specificity is the #1 reason
Simulated illustration of the low-prevalence regime.
Sources — [33] Liu, UAD survey 2026 (limited clinical use) · [6] AutoDDPM · [7] THOR, MICCAI 2024
Tension ② · reconstruction's paradox

Erase the lesion, or keep the anatomy?

  • Too little noise → lesion survives → false negatives (sensitivity↓)
  • Too much noise → healthy anatomy destroyed → false positives (specificity↓)
  • Early fix: just tune the noise level (AnoDDPM's Simplex) — but you can't win both
  • → so the field switched to guidance (next)
too little
lesion still visible → FN
just right
lesion gone, anatomy intact
too much
healthy cortex erased → FP
Tuning the noise level trades false negatives for false positives — no single level wins. Simulated · not a real case.
Sources — [5] AnoDDPM (Simplex), CVPRW 2022 · [6] AutoDDPM 2023
Tension ② · responses

Guidance & latent restoration

  • THOR (MICCAI'24): guides denoising with intermediate anomaly maps → keeps healthy detail, erases only the lesion → FP↓
  • cDDPM (2025): conditions the denoiser on the input's latent → patient-specific, won't replicate pathology
  • Latent + rectified flow (3D, 2025): restore in a VAE latent → volumetric context, far fewer steps
  • ⇒ guidance cuts false positives but doesn't close specificity — improved, not solved
THOR · guided restoration
DDPM (top) vs THOR (guided): the guided trajectory keeps healthy tissue → residual = the true lesion (GT).

Tension ② · the other half

Feature / VLM: no paradox, specificity stays open

✓ No noise paradox

Embedding / VLM don't reconstruct → no "add-noise-destroys-anatomy" dilemma.

✗ A different bar

No obvious operating point for a distance score; thresholds drift across sites.

Takeaway: specificity (②) is a bar for all methods; the noise paradox is only reconstruction's special case.

Tension ③

Scarcity & generalization

Two problems — scarcity (no abnormal data) + generalization (many modalities)

Tension ③ · Self-supervision

Manufacture the anomalies you lack

  • CutPaste · FPI · PII (2020–21): synthesize defects → proxy segmentation, no real anomalies
  • Limit synthetic ≠ real (learns paste artifacts, doesn't transfer)
  • Latest ART-ASyn: anatomy-aware, realistic texture (CXR, 2025)
  • ⇒ answers scarcity only (usually single-modality); trained on fake defects → a detector, not an explanation
ART-ASyn · anatomy-aware synthesis
ART-ASyn: lung segment → brush dynamics → drawn anomaly → realistic CXR anomaly + mask.
Sources — [9] CutPaste, CVPR 2021 · [10] PII · [11] FPI · [32] ART-ASyn 2025
Tension ③ · VLM adaptation

Borrow a foundation model — MVFA-AD

  • Why CLIP fails: its features are image-level & semantic; medical anomalies are local & subtle
  • MVFA's fix: frozen CLIP + lightweight adapters at multiple levels + pixel-level V–L alignment → localize (AS), not just classify (AC)
  • Two branches: zero-shot (text) + few-shot (memory bank), ensembled
  • ⇒ cross-modality generalization comes from here — not self-supervision

Tension ③ · VLM adaptation

MediCLIP → MadCLIP & the FM trend

  • MediCLIP / MVFA (2024): few-shot, no real anomalies — synthetic tasks + learnable prompts + adapters
  • Latest MadCLIP: beats MVFA cross-dataset (MICCAI '25)
  • ⇒ even 2025 SOTA is a better detector, not a better explainer → interpretability is the frontier
  • VLM is also a "represent normal" (①); filed under ③ as it mainly solves scarcity/cross-modality

Reality check · evaluation

The bug is in the evaluation, not the model

MedIAnomaly's core message: the field lacks a fair, comprehensive evaluation — which muddies conclusions and holds it back. [15]

How to read the chart → on easy datasets a plain AE already scores ~99% — the metric is maxed out, so it can't tell methods apart. On hard datasets the same AE swings 37–68% — that's where real differences show.

The conclusion: much reported "SOTA" gain is an evaluation artifact — chasing saturated datasets, not real progress.

Barriers to clinical translation

What still blocks the clinic

Interpretability · 3D · trust

Translational barrier ① · interpretability

A heatmap is not a diagnosis

Genuinely hard: a map shows where — not what, how sure, or why.

brain MRI → anomaly heatmap
A heatmap over a brain MRI flags where the deviation is — but names nothing, gives no calibrated confidence, and can't say why. [31]

The barrier

A heatmap is indirect evidence — no name, uncalibrated score. Saliency can even be unfaithful: some maps barely change when the model is randomized [36].

What would help

Treat it as a lead, not a verdict: test faithfulness [36], calibrate into clinical confidence, move to named explanations [35]. It's a regulatory requirement [37].

Sources — [31] cDDPM, CBM 2025 (brain-MRI figure) · [35] XAD survey, arXiv:2302.06670 · [36] Adebayo, Sanity Checks, NeurIPS 2018 · [37] Ahmed, XAI-in-medical review, BMC 2025 · [33] Liu, UAD survey 2026
Translational barrier ② · 3D / volumetric

Don't fake 3D by stacking 2D slices

Clinicians read the whole volume, but most methods score each 2D slice alone — losing 3D context. The fix isn't a giant 3D model; it's doing 3D efficiently.

31.4
3D-VAE Dice beats its 2D version's 25.8 (+5.6) — volumetric context helps. [38]
~10×
faster in latent space — full-3D becomes affordable. [26]

3D UAD is now its own benchmark — 4,365 brain-MRI + 376 liver-CT, 21 algorithms. [34]

The barrier

2D slice-by-slice loses volumetric context — a lesion's 3D shape vanishes, so "normal" is harder to model; full 3D generation is compute-bound [39].

What would help

Not a bigger model — reconstruct in a compact latent space: ~10× faster [26], making true 3D context affordable.

Sources — [38] Bengs, 3D spatial-erasing (brain MRI), IJCARS 2021 · [26] Latent Rectified Flow (3D), Front. Radiol. 2026 · [34] 3D UAD survey & benchmark 2026 · [39] Behrendt, brain-MRI UAD review, MedIA 2026
Translational barrier ③ · trust

A high AUROC is not "deployable"

A high balanced-set AUROC ≠ deployable — at real prevalence, false alarms drown the true findings:

1,000 scans at ~1% prevalence → only ~10 true lesions. Even 95% specificity fires on 5% of 990 normals → ~50 false alarms → 5 false : 1 true → alert fatigue.
Illustrative arithmetic — not paper data.

The barrier

Beyond false positives: cross-site domain shift is a known OOD hazard [40], pixel labels are scarce, and privacy & governance [25]. So far no unsupervised AD is FDA-cleared [33].

What would help

Ask up front: how many FPs at 1% prevalence? Holds across scanners / sites? [40] Who owns privacy & monitoring? [25] Design for it — don't bolt it on.

Sources — [33] Liu, UAD survey 2026 · [40] "First, Do No Harm" OOD in medicine 2025 · [25] Bourtoule, Machine Unlearning, S&P 2021
Summary

Roadmap & takeaways

Near-term

Standardized benchmarks · latent-diffusion / flow baselines · FM adapters (DINOv2 / MadCLIP).

Long-term

Reasoning-grounded, trustworthy, 3D-native systems.

  • Medical AD = learn-normal, flag-deviation; two engines: diffusion & VLM adaptation
  • Real bottlenecks: specificity, 3D, trust, privacy — not raw accuracy
Honest note: surveys taxonomize by 4 method families [15][33]; this talk re-groups into three tensions and promotes specificity to axis ②. The framing is ours; the methods and challenges come from the surveys.
