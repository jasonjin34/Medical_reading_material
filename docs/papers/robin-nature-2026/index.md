# A multi-agent system for automating scientific discovery

> **Bibkey** `Ghareeb_2026` · **Venue** Nature (2026) · **Category** agents · **Relevance** high · **Access** open
> **Link** <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10652-y> · `status: complete`

---

## One-liner
FutureHouse's Robin chains literature agents (Crow/Falcon, built on PaperQA2) and a data-analysis agent (Finch) into a lab-in-the-loop cycle: given a disease, it proposes mechanisms, in vitro models and drug candidates; humans run the suggested experiments and upload raw data; Finch runs eight independent analysis trajectories and takes a consensus; Robin updates its hypotheses. For dry AMD it proposed enhancing RPE phagocytosis, found the ROCK inhibitor ripasudil (1.89-fold over DMSO) and KL001, and via RNA-seq uncovered ABCA1 upregulation — all hypotheses, experimental directions, data analyses and data figures in the main text were produced by Robin.

## Problem
Drug repurposing history shows insights sitting in the literature for years before becoming treatments (dabrafenib otoprotection ~10 years, ketamine 22, KarXT 13). LLM systems can generate hypotheses (multi-agent decomposition, domain fine-tuning, tools, human feedback), but **none automates all the key intellectual steps** — generating hypotheses and experimental strategies, analysing lab results, and refining hypotheses in light of new data. Robin aims to join literature-driven hypothesis generation and autonomous analysis of experimental data into one continuous feedback system.

## Method
**Components**: Crow (concise literature review) and Falcon (deep literature reports), both built on PaperQA2 with access to scientific literature, clinical-trial reports and Open Targets; Finch, an Aviary-based, ReAct-style Jupyter-native analysis agent writing and executing Python/R line by line in a prebuilt Docker image (BixBench-env:v1.0) with only two tools, edit_cell and submit_answer. Robin itself uses OpenAI o4-mini for synthesis and hypothesis generation and Claude 3.7 Sonnet as the pairwise judge. **Hypothesis generation**: pose general disease questions → Crow answers → derive 10 candidate causal mechanisms → Crow writes an in vitro model report per mechanism → LLM-judge pairwise ranking → pick the top assay → same flow proposes 30 candidate drugs → Falcon writes an evaluation report per drug → LLM-judge tournament ranking. ≤25 hypotheses use a full round robin, >25 sample 300 random pairs; a Bradley–Terry–Luce model estimates strengths and mitigates position bias; the judge prompt was generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro from expert pairwise comparisons. **Data analysis**: upload .FCS (flow) or gene counts (RNA-seq) with an analysis prompt; Finch runs eight independent trajectories in parallel, and a meta-analysis synthesizes a consensus. The authors noticed the agentic Robin almost always called tools in the same order, so they rewrote it as a deterministic Jupyter-notebook workflow for stability.

## Data
**Experimental systems**: round one used ARPE-19 cells with pHrodo beads in a flow-cytometry phagocytosis assay (Robin had proposed primary/stem-cell RPE and photoreceptor outer segments; substituted for availability); RNA-seq (ARPE-19 + Y-27632); a second round of 10 drugs; then a re-screen in primary RPE stem cells (RPE-SCs) from a donor over 60 with fluorescently labelled bovine rod outer segments (ROSs), plus LDH cytotoxicity, ELISA and immunostaining. **Benchmark data**: 170 drug-discovery-relevant BixBench questions (131 bioinformatics, 39 biostatistics, 38 data capsules). Raw RNA-seq is in SRA (PRJNA1464762).

## Key results
- **Discovery chain**: reviewing 151 papers yielded 10 dAMD mechanisms and selected "enhance RPE phagocytosis" with a flow assay; reviewing ~400 more produced 30 candidates. Round one tested the top five (exendin-4, fingolimod, MFGE8 as positive control, Y-27632, AICAR+TUDCA); Y-27632 was the hit, matching human analysis.
- **RNA-seq**: Robin proposed sequencing Y-27632-treated cells; Finch found ~3-fold ABCA1 upregulation (adjusted P = 2.13×10⁻⁸³), with the same DE genes identified in >50% of the eight trajectories; GO enrichment pointed to actin organization, small-GTPase signalling and autophagy.
- **Round two**: of 10 new candidates, ripasudil (a ROCK inhibitor approved for glaucoma in Japan) raised phagocytosis **1.89-fold** (human analysis 1.75-fold) and was more potent than Y-27632 in dose–response; it replicated in RPE-SCs with ROSs, with LDH release falling as dose rose; KL001 (a CRY-stabilizing clock modulator) was also a hit in RPE-SCs; ripasudil also upregulated ABCA1 in RPE-SCs.
- **Ablation (reference hallucination)**: replacing Falcon, or Crow+Falcon, with o4-mini sharply increased hallucinated references in drug proposals; across 15 assay proposals Crow produced no hallucinated references while **44.5 ± 6.37%** of o4-mini's were hallucinated. All three ablations ranked significantly worse in an LLM-judged tournament.
- **Finch evaluation**: on expert rubrics, 86% for the round-two RNA-seq task and 100% for flow (three runs each); on 170 BixBench questions Finch scored **22.8 ± 1.7%** vs 1.6 ± 1.2% for bare Claude 3.7 Sonnet; biostatistics 47.9% far above bioinformatics 15.3% (multi-step pipelines are parameter-sensitive). Failures are classed as wrong answer, distractor match, formatting, unsure, and empty/failed.
- **Against OpenAI Deep Research**: asked for 19 candidates with the same prompt, Deep Research produced 17 unique drugs with **zero hits** in the RPE-SC assay, and never suggested ROCK inhibition.
- **Judge and cost**: on average 7.25 of the LLM judge's top-10 matched the experts' top-10; on repeated identical pairs the judge was self-consistent 88% of the time vs 61% for human experts. A full run (45 Crow + 30 Falcon calls) costs ~US$10.76; ~551 papers synthesized in ~30 min (est. ~294 human hours), cutting a cycle's cognitive labour from an estimated 359–424 hours to under 2.

## Contributions
- Among the first systems to close the loop between literature-driven hypothesis generation and autonomous analysis of experimental data, iteratively discovering therapeutic candidates in vitro.
- **Multi-trajectory consensus analysis**: harnessing LLM stochasticity — eight independent trajectories plus a meta-analysis — turning run-to-run variation from a bug into a stability mechanism.
- LLM-judge ranking via distributed pairwise comparisons with a BTL model, with judge–expert agreement and self-consistency quantified.
- Targeted ablations (the retrieval agents' effect on reference hallucination) and a wet-lab-level comparison against Deep Research.

## Limitations
- Only semi-autonomous: humans run experiments, often deviating from Robin's proposals (cell line, substrate); Finch analyses flow data, but RNA-seq demultiplexing and alignment were done by a human.
- Produces experimental outlines, not precise executable protocols; Finch depends on expert-engineered task prompts and, without them (BixBench), reaches only 22.8%, especially weak on multi-step bioinformatics.
- One disease, in vitro only; ripasudil and KL001 need in vivo models and ultimately randomized trials; the ABCA1/autophagy mechanism remains to be validated.
- Several evaluations rely on an LLM judge (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) whose prompt was itself LLM-generated from expert judgements.

## Relation to our direction
Robin is a ready template for the **revert stage's "nominate → wet lab → re-nominate" loop**: disease phenotype (reduced RPE phagocytosis) → candidate perturbations → in vitro readout → mechanistic omics (RNA-seq) → a new target (ABCA1). Transposed to our direction: the detect stage provides anomalous kidney regions and programs, the model stage predicts perturbation effects in the virtual tissue, a Robin-style loop merges literature evidence with those predictions into candidates, and a Finch-style agent analyses the post-experiment readouts. For **Topic 2 (trustworthy agents)** it supplies three directly usable empirical results: **(1) rerun stability** — the authors state Finch gives different results on identical prompts and data and hedge with eight trajectories plus consensus; this is exactly the rerun stability Topic 2 wants to quantify, and ">50% of trajectories agree" is a ready-made stability metric generalizable to a per-claim agreement rate; **(2) citation hallucination** — 44.5% of o4-mini's references in proposals were hallucinated while PaperQA2-style retrieval agents drove this to zero, giving the claim-to-evidence verifier a sub-task with a known baseline (citation existence/support checks); **(3) omics-specific failures** — multi-step bioinformatics on BixBench (15.3%) is far worse than single-step statistics (47.9%), and failures are classed into wrong answer, distractor match, formatting, unsure and empty/failed — a starting point for our omics-specific failure taxonomy, to be refined with snRNA/snATAC/Xenium-specific errors (ambient RNA, doublets, segmentation errors producing spurious differential expression). One more caution: the LLM judge is 88% self-consistent vs 61% for experts — more consistent is not more correct, so verifier calibration in Topic 2 must report consistency and accuracy separately.
