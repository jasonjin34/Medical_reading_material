## Abstract
Spatial biomarkers are critical for precision oncology but remain challenging to systematically discover due to the complexity of whole-slide images. We present PathPrism, an interpretable AI framework for spatial biomarker discovery and virtual experimentation. Unlike black-box models, PathPrism encodes tissue architecture into pathologically informed spatial features, enabling transparent modeling of prognosis, molecular alterations, and therapy response. Applied to 7,000 patients with colorectal cancer across 11 cohorts, PathPrism uncovered hundreds of biomarkers predictive of survival, MSI, BRAF, and TP53 mutations, and stratified chemotherapy benefit in stage II/III disease. Building on these interpretable findings, PathPrism uses large language models as auxiliary tools to generate hypotheses grounded in spatial semantics. We further introduce VirtualWSI, a platform for semantic perturbation within an interpretable spatial biomarker atlas. PathPrism provides a scalable and interpretable framework for spatial biomarker discovery.

### Introduction
Histopathological biomarkers are central to cancer diagnosis, prognosis estimation, and treatment selection. Spatial biomarkers, defined by multiscale and multimodal patterns within the tumor microenvironment (TME), hold particular promise but are difficult to discover because tissue heterogeneity, complex spatial organization, and subtle visual patterns often exceed the capacity of human interpretation. As a result, only a small number of spatial biomarkers have been clinically validated. Most deep-learning (DL) models in computational pathology remain black boxes; post-hoc explanations derived from raw pixels or latent image representations are diffuse and ambiguous, making it hard to define, quantify, characterize, and validate signals as pathologically interpretable biomarkers. This limits clinical translation, LLM-assisted hypothesis generation, and the transition from prediction to active perturbation. The authors present PathPrism, an AI-driven framework for interpretable spatial biomarker discovery, coupled with structure-preserving semantic perturbation to explore the spatial biomarker atlas.

### Overview of PathPrism
The core principle is to maximize interpretability without compromising performance, via biologically informed input representations and a transparent, simplified model design.
- (A) PrismNet acts like a prism, producing quantitatively defined, pathologically interpretable segmentation maps. PrismNet is a DL model trained on 100,000 annotated CRC histopathology patches for high-throughput semantic segmentation into eight tissue types: adipose (ADI), debris (DEB), lymphocytes (LYM), mucus (MUC), smooth muscle (MUS), normal colon mucosa (NORM), cancer-associated stroma (STR), and colorectal adenocarcinoma epithelium (TUM).
- (B) MacroNet is trained to associate macroscopic tissue architecture with patient survival, and is interpreted from multiple angles to reveal predictive spatial patterns.
- (C) Guided by MacroNet, the semantic maps undergo a second-level decomposition into quantified, human-interpretable spatial features (tissue spatial fractions, spatial entropy, slide-level graph features) — analogous to a prism refracting light into a spectrum.
- (D) Minimal, transparent models use this spectrum to predict prognosis, molecular alterations, and treatment response with direct feature-level attribution.
- (E) LLM-assisted analysis organizes selected biomarkers into structured hypotheses for expert review.
- (F) VirtualWSI performs semantic perturbation for model interrogation within spatial biomarker space.

### PathPrism efficiently links semantics and prognosis
On CRC100K (trained on NCT-CRC-HE-100K-NONORM, tested on CRC-VAL-HE-7K), benchmarking of foundation models and dimensionality-reduction strategies identified UNI features + PCA as optimal. PrismNet achieved macro-average F1 = 0.948 (95% CI 0.942–0.954), MCC = 0.958 (0.952–0.964), macro-AUROC = 0.988 (0.986–0.990). PrismNet remained robust with partially annotated data, enabling efficient transfer to new cancer types with minimal labeling.
MacroNet was trained to learn associations between macroscopic tissue architecture and disease-specific survival (DSS) directly from segmentation outputs. In 5-fold cross-validation on DACHS, MacroNet achieved mean C-index = 0.716 ± 0.020, comparable to SOTA foundation models (attMIL-UNI, GigaPath, CHIEF, COBRA, PRISM). Substituting PrismNet-derived maps with baseline segmentations reduced performance. On external cohorts, MacroNet ranked top-two in MCO and CR07; Kaplan-Meier risk stratification gave HRs of 3.68 (MCO), 2.48 (CR07), 3.27 (TCGA).

### Spatial biomarker discovery via interpretation
Stratifying patients into high-/low-risk groups by MacroNet score: high-risk patients showed increased TUM, DEB, STR; low-risk enriched for ADI, LYM, MUS, NORM. Saliency-based attribution showed greater TUM and MUC contributions in high-risk despite comparable proportions; LYM and MUS showed higher abundance and attribution in low-risk. LYM and STR frequently co-occurred in low-risk cases (cooperative favorable pattern). A structure-preserving counterfactual explanation framework showed that amplifying LYM consistently reduced risk scores; increasing NORM and LYM reduced predicted risk in high-risk patients, whereas increasing MUC, TUM, or STR elevated predicted risk in low-risk patients. Low-risk samples displayed orderly architecture (MUS separating TUM and ADI, abundant inflammation/INF near MUS); high-risk samples showed disorganized STR/TUM structures with TUM invading beyond MUS into ADI and often lacking INF, with significantly higher spatial entropy and fragmented attribution across STR/TUM/ADI (aligning with SARIFA-like infiltration). Low-risk attribution concentrated along the TUM–MUS boundary and INF zones.

### Potential biomarker characterization and verification
Insights were translated into testable hypotheses and quantified across spatial fraction, spatial entropy, and graph-based topology. Slide-level graphs encode contiguous tissue regions and local proximity / inter-tissue relationships (intra- and inter-tissue characterization). In total, 628 interpretable spatial features were compiled. Evaluated across DACHS, MCO, CR07, TCGA: several STR- and TUM-related single-tissue biomarkers had C-indices >0.6 (approaching 0.7). TUM graph entropy: C-index 0.62, HR 1.97; MUS spatial fraction: C-index 0.60, HR 0.57. Multi-tissue graph biomarkers: ADI-TUM-E in CR07 reached C-index 0.69; STR-TUM-EN1 and MUS-TUM-EN2 in TCGA each 0.73. Favorable biomarkers often involved LYM-MUS/LYM-STR (DACHS) and LYM-NORM (TCGA); poor prognosis with STR-TUM and ADI-TUM interactions.

### Spatial biomarker spectrum enables transparent prediction
The 628-feature spectrum functions as an alternative to foundation-model slide-level embeddings, but each feature is mathematically defined and linked to semantic tissue maps. An elastic-net-regularized Cox model (Coxnet) trained on all 628 features gave the spatial prognostic model (SPM), refined to a filtered SPM (FSPM) using the top 10 features. In 5-fold CV on DACHS, both exceeded C-index 0.7, matching MacroNet and leading foundation models, and generalized to MCO/CR07/TCGA. Predictions were directly linked to features such as LYM–STR and ADI–TUM interactions, stromal area fraction, and tumor centrality (TUM–AC). Combining the 10 FSPM features with foundation-model embeddings improved performance, with spatial biomarkers contributing nearly 25% of overall feature importance.
For mutations: an L1-regularized logistic regression predicted MSI with median AUC 0.85 (5-fold CV, DACHS) and 0.78 across seven external cohorts; key features included MUC-TUM spatial isolation (MUC-TUM-IR2) and debris entropy (DEB-ENT). Trained on CORSA/EPIC/WHI/IWHS/CRA and tested on TCGA, spatial features predicted BMPR2, BRAF, and TP53 mutations with AUCs 0.78, 0.75, 0.66. Macro-scale spatial organization encodes genetic information traditionally assumed to require cellular-level analysis.

### Interpretable spatial biomarkers uncover divergent responses to adjuvant chemotherapy (ACT)
Stage II ACT-treated: LYM-TUM-AD2 distinguished outcomes in MCO (HR 5.19, p=0.0005), generalized to HECOG (HR 2.00, p=0.007). Stage III ACT-treated: TUM-NN stratified risk in MCO (HR 1.74, p=0.021), significant in HECOG, IDEA Arm A/B. Using a Cox interaction-based framework: in MCO stage II, ACT associated with worse outcomes overall (HR 2.53, p=0.001, likely selection bias); stratifying by LYM-NORM-ED, patients with low LYM-NORM-ED (≤0.038) showed no adverse ACT association (HR 0.79), whereas high (>0.038; HR 4.49) or absent LYM-NORM interaction (HR 3.35) had worse ACT outcomes. In stage III (ACT usually uniformly beneficial), LYM–MUC–CC (fragmentation of LYM connectivity around mucinous regions) revealed striking heterogeneity: high fragmentation (>5) → ACT worse survival (HR 9.08, p=0.008); low fragmentation (≤5) → significant ACT benefit (HR 0.29, p<0.0001); interaction HR 32.45, p=0.001.

### Translating cohort-level spatial signatures into hypotheses through LLMs
LLMs organized cohort-level spatial signatures into structured hypotheses for expert review. For LYM-MUC-CC, prompts grounded in feature definitions and H&E morphology suggested fragmented LYM connectivity around mucinous regions reflects immune exclusion and reduced ACT benefit; a second LLM reviewer added chemokine sequestration, hypoxia-driven immune remodeling, and myeloid suppression. Similar workflow applied to DEB entropy and MUC-TUM interaction. Five CRC pathology/tumor-biology experts rated most hypotheses as biologically coherent and experimentally actionable (not experimental validation). PathPrism-tailored prompts produced more consistent outputs than coarse prompts.

### Perturbation-based VirtualWSI enables exploration of the spatial biomarker atlas
VirtualWSI is an in-silico semantic perturbation framework for model interrogation, enabling WSI-wide channel perturbation and interactive perturbation of selected regions while preserving global tissue structure. A spatial biomarker atlas (UMAP) of CRC patients was constructed from the spectrum. MSI-H and BRAF-mutant tumors localized to overlapping regions, opposite to MUC–TUM–IR2. Among MCO stage III patients, LYM–MUC–CC subgroups formed structured clusters; regions enriched for ACT-associated harm had higher LYM–MUC–CC. Perturbing MUC and LYM channels produced progressive, continuous trajectories across the atlas; mean LYM–MUC–CC increased with perturbation strength; responses fell into positive/flat/negative archetypes. Perturbation-induced state transitions served as a dynamic biomarker: applied to patients without detectable LYM–MUC structure, it further separated a subgroup with significant ACT benefit (HR 0.27, p=0.010). Region-level perturbation of individual samples (LYM-to-MUS replacement, STR expansion, TUM extension into ADI) showed FSPM risk changed minimally after LYM removal but rose with STR-driven boundary disruption and disorganized TUM-ADI invasion.

### Transferability to breast cancer (BRCA)
A BRCA-specific dataset trained PrismNet for segmentation; MacroNet/SPM/FSPM performed comparably to leading foundation models; LYM-STR and NEC-STR were most influential. Among trastuzumab-deruxtecan (T-DXd)-treated patients, STR-ACL was associated with survival (HR 6.11, p=0.018) and inversely correlated with ERBB2, positively with TOP1 expression. Among sacituzumab-govitecan (SG)-treated patients, higher ADI-NEC-BT2 associated with improved outcomes (HR 0.06, p=0.002) and positively correlated with TACSTD2 (not TOP1). Correlations were modest, suggesting spatial biomarkers capture tumor-context features beyond target gene expression alone.

### Discussion
PathPrism addresses the interpretability/performance trade-off through a transparent, semantically structured framework that encodes WSIs into pathologically meaningful spatial representations (tissue identities + spatial relationships), enabling spatial patterns to be quantified, modeled, visualized, perturbed, and explored in a common language shared by models and experts. It points toward computational pathology where AI builds interpretable representations for hypothesis-driven research, connecting prediction, hypothesis generation, perturbation-based exploration, and spatial atlases of disease states. The architecture is compatible with emerging modalities: spatial multi-omics, 3D pathology, and multimodal integration; future applications may include therapeutic response modeling, drug discovery, and agentic AI workflows.

### Limitations of the study
Depends on predefined semantic tissue representations, so it is constrained to patterns captured by the selected segmentation categories; new tumor types require cancer-specific annotations, PrismNet training, and validation (as in BRCA). VirtualWSI perturbations should be interpreted as exploratory model interrogation, not biological causality or physically realizable tissue states. Prognostic performance is in a moderate range, so downstream biological interpretations (including ACT findings) are hypothesis-generating and require further validation. The LLM component organizes hypotheses for expert review but does not generate validated biological insight without experimental confirmation.
