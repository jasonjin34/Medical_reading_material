# The effect of background noise and its removal on the analysis of single-cell expression data

> **Bibkey** `Janssen_2023` · **Venue** Genome Biology (2023) · **Category** single-cell · **Relevance** high · **Access** open
> **Link** <https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-02978-x> · `status: complete`

---

## One-liner
Kidney cells from two mouse subspecies (*M. m. castaneus* CAST/EiJ and *M. m. domesticus* C57BL/6J and 129S1/SvImJ) are pooled in one 10x channel so that subspecies-diagnostic homozygous SNPs separate endogenous from foreign molecules, giving a **genotype-based ground truth for the per-cell background-noise fraction ρ_cell**. Background noise varies widely between replicates and cells (3–35% of UMIs on average), comes mostly from ambient RNA rather than barcode swapping, and — used as ground truth to benchmark CellBender / DecontX / SoupX — CellBender gives the most precise estimates and the biggest gain for marker genes, while clustering and classification are fairly robust to background noise.

## Problem
In droplet-based scRNA-seq / snRNA-seq not every read on a cell barcode comes from the encapsulated cell: cell-free **ambient RNA** from broken cells and **barcode swapping** (chimeric molecules) during library PCR both add background noise. It makes marker genes appear in the wrong cell types, shrinks log2FC, can suggest spurious "novel" cell types, and can create false positives in between-condition DE. Existing decontamination tools (SoupX, DecontX, CellBender) are usually evaluated on a human–mouse cell-line mix (cross-species contamination in non-overlapping features, low cell-type diversity) or on exclusive marker genes — neither captures the **shifts in expression level of the same gene** that dominate in real tissue. The paper asks how large, variable and where-from background noise is, how much it affects downstream analysis, and whether the tools can estimate and remove it without genotype information.

## Method
**Design**: each 10x channel (Chromium Next GEM 3' v3.1, targeting 10,000 cells/nuclei) pools kidney quarters from three mice (CAST/EiJ, C57BL/6J, 129S1/SvImJ; 6–8 weeks, PBS-perfused). scRNA-seq: fresh tissue, Liberase TH dissociation; snRNA-seq: RNAlater-preserved frozen tissue, TST nuclei isolation (nuc3 had two extra washes vs nuc2). Cell Ranger 3.0.2 (mm10 2020A for sc, pre-mRNA mm10 for sn) → cells = DropletUtils `barcodeRanks` inflection ∪ Cell Ranger calls → cellsnp-lite (≥20 UMIs, MAF ≥0.1) + Vireo against the Mouse Genomes Project VCF, dropping doublets/unassigned → SingleR with Denisenko et al. mouse kidney reference (13 cell types). **Background estimate**: using only homozygous subspecies-diagnostic SNPs, an adaptation of souporcell (Heaton et al.): the observable contamination probability per locus is p = ρ_c · A_{l,1}/(A_{l,0}+A_{l,1}), where the experiment-wide (cells + empty droplets) foreign-allele fraction extrapolates to unseen same-genotype contamination; ρ_c is the binomial-likelihood MLE by 1-D optimisation on [0,1], with profile-likelihood 95% CIs. Main analyses use only CAST cells (9–43% of cells; their contamination comes mostly from domesticus and is therefore largely visible), excluding mitochondrial genes. **Source analysis**: domesticus-allele counts form endogenous (domesticus cells), contamination (CAST cells) and empty (plateau barcodes with ~500–1000 UMIs) pseudobulk profiles, compared by Spearman correlation, SCDC deconvolution and intron fraction; PCR chimeras = one CB-UMI mapping to several genes. **Benchmark**: CellBender v0.2.0 (fpr 0.01; also 0.05/0.1), SoupX 1.6.1 (autoEstCont; also fixed 0.1/0.2), DecontX (celda 1.12.0; default vs empty-droplet background). Per-cell estimate a_c = 1 − Σx/Σr vs ρ_c by RMSLE and Kendall τ; downstream: marker detection rate/log2FC, SingleR prediction score, silhouette, cluster purity, and k-NN (k=50) overlap with a "genotype-cleaned" matrix.

## Data
Five 10x channels: scRNA-seq **rep1, rep2, rep3** (fresh tissue) and snRNA-seq **nuc2, nuc3** (frozen tissue, from the same samples as rep2 and rep3 respectively). Each experiment yields 5,000–20,000 good cells, 9–43% CAST; the text also cites rep3 "3900 cells" and rep2 "15,000 cells" (inconsistent with the 5,000 lower bound — likely a different filter; check Fig. 1B). >40,000 informative SNPs (~32,000 separate the subspecies, ~10,000 the two domesticus strains); ~1,000 informative SNPs covered per cell (~20% of UMIs), only ~100 in nuc2; ~7,000 genes carry ≥1 subspecies-informative SNP. Proximal tubule (PT) is 66–89% of cells. Raw data: GEO GSE218853 (GSM6757771–75, raw and filtered `feature_bc_matrix.h5` per sample; SRA PRJNA906016); Zenodo 7328632 (one zip per replicate with Seurat objects, the per-cell contamination RDS and raw/filtered h5); code at GitHub Hellmann-Lab/scRNA-seq_Contamination (GPL-3.0; Zenodo 7941521).

## Key results
(1) **Size and variability**: median foreign-genotype count fraction per experiment 2–27% (visible lower bound); extrapolated median ρ_cell: rep3 mostly <3%, rep2 ~11%, nuc2 35%, nuc3 17% (rep1 not stated in the text; see Fig. 2A); frozen-tissue snRNA-seq is much higher than the matching scRNA-seq (35% vs 11%, 17% vs 3%) and unrelated to cell yield. The **absolute amount of contaminating UMIs is roughly constant across cells**, so larger cells have smaller fractions. (2) **Source**: contaminating UMIs per cell match UMIs in empty droplets; in sc the contamination profile correlates with both empty (ρ=0.73–0.85) and endogenous (ρ=0.70–0.87), in sn it resembles empty (~0.85 vs ~0.50) and empty droplets show a reduced PT share; the intron fraction of contamination is close to empty; median PCR-chimera fraction 0.2% (rep3)–0.7% (nuc3), median swapping contribution <10% of background → mostly ambient RNA. (3) **Markers**: PT marker Slc34a1 detected in ~60% of non-PT cells in rep2; higher background → smaller log2FC. (4) **Estimation accuracy**: in sc, CellBender ≈ DecontX, SoupX underestimates and misses cell-to-cell variation; in sn, SoupX gets global levels better but still no per-cell correlation, CellBender/DecontX worsen; CellBender is most robust at defaults (cost ~45 CPU h per channel); all methods worst on nuc3, which is dropped thereafter. (5) **Markers after correction**: rep2 Slc34a1 non-PT detection 60% → SoupX 54%, CellBender 7%, DecontX_background 9%, DecontX_default 2%; in nuc2 DecontX_default fails (87%), DecontX_background 20%, CellBender 4%, SoupX <1%; CellBender gives the largest log2FC gain for known markers. (6) **Classification/clustering**: <1% (sc) / up to 1.3% (nuc2) of cells change label; DecontX yields the purest sc clusters but lowers k-NN overlap with the genotype ground truth (distorts fine structure), SoupX leaves fine structure mostly unchanged; at nuc2's high background all methods improve. **Recommendation**: always decontaminate for marker analysis; for classification/clustering/pseudotime only when background is high.

## Contributions
- The first experimental dataset giving per-cell background-fraction ground truth in a **real multi-cell-type tissue** and on the **same gene features** (subspecies pooling + homozygous SNPs).
- Multiple lines of evidence (three pseudobulk profiles, intron fraction, PCR chimeras) that background is mostly ambient RNA.
- A ground-truth benchmark of CellBender / DecontX / SoupX on per-cell estimates and downstream effects, including the k-NN view that decontamination can distort fine structure.
- Covers low (rep3), medium (rep2) and high (nuc2) background regimes and releases raw matrices plus per-cell estimates.

## Limitations
- One tissue (mouse kidney), one platform (10x 3' v3.1), five channels; PT is 66–89% so composition is skewed; nuc2 and nuc3 differ in protocol (wash steps), so they are not strict replicates.
- Ground truth is reliable only for CAST cells; same-genotype contamination is extrapolated (assuming SNP-covering counts represent the whole cell), and nuc2 has only ~100 informative SNPs per cell, so CIs are wider.
- Healthy tissue only; no disease states, so it cannot show how background noise interacts with real disease-related state changes.
- Only three tools at fixed versions (e.g. CellBender v0.2.0); conclusions are for defaults, and SoupX improves markedly when given the true background level (Fig. S15).
- Per-replicate cell counts in the text are not fully consistent (5,000–20,000 vs rep3 "3900 cells"); the supplementary figures are needed to confirm.

## Relation to our direction
Sits on the **artifact side of the detect stage** (Plan A, track A2: QC & artifacts). Plan A builds a **human-kidney single-cell benchmark** testing whether anomaly detectors separate disease-related anomalies from measurement artifacts such as ambient RNA. This paper is used **only to calibrate the strength and profile of the ambient RNA we inject**: (1) strength — the empirical distribution of per-cell background fractions (median ~3–11% in fresh-tissue sc, ~17–35% in frozen-tissue sn; per-cell ρ_c in each replicate's `perCell_noMito_CAST_binom.RDS` on Zenodo), plus the finding that the absolute number of contaminating UMIs is roughly constant across cells (fraction falls with cell size) — a direct guide for the injection model (fixed absolute amount vs fixed fraction); (2) profile — the background profile should come from **empty droplets**, not the mean of cells (in sn, contamination correlates ~0.85 with empty vs ~0.50 with endogenous), and the sn ambient profile has fewer PT transcripts and a lower intron fraction; the raw `feature_bc_matrix.h5` files provide the empty droplets needed. (3) Evaluation design to borrow: RMSLE + Kendall τ against an artifact ground truth, and k-NN overlap to test whether decontamination harms fine structure — mirroring our need to check that artifact injection/removal does not mask or fake disease signal. Caveat: it is **healthy mouse** kidney, not usable as human disease benchmark data; transferring the strength across species/platform should be re-checked on empty droplets from human kidney data (e.g. KPMP).
