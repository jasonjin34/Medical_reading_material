# Deep contrastive learning enables genome-wide virtual screening

- **Authors:** Jia, Yinjun; Gao, Bowen; Tan, Jiaxin; Zheng, Jiqing; Hong, Xin; Zhu, Wenyu; Tan, Haichuan; Xiao, Yuan; Tan, Liping; Cai, Hongyi; Huang, Yanwen; Deng, Zhiheng; Wu, Xiangwei; Jin, Yue; Yuan, Yafei; Tian, Jiekang; He, Wei; Ma, Weiying; Zhang, Yaqin; Liu, Lei; Yan, Chuangye; Zhang, Wei; Lan, Yanyan

## Abstract
Recent breakthroughs in protein structure prediction have opened new avenues for genome-wide drug discovery, yet existing virtual screening methods remain computationally prohibitive. We present DrugCLIP, a contrastive learning framework that achieves ultrafast and accurate virtual screening, up to 10 million times faster than docking, while consistently outperforming various baselines on in silico benchmarks. In wet-lab validations, DrugCLIP achieved a 15% hit rate for norepinephrine transporter, and structures of two identified inhibitors were determined in complex with the target protein. For thyroid hormone receptor interactor 12, a target that lacks holo structures and small-molecule binders, DrugCLIP achieved a 17.5% hit rate using only AlphaFold2-predicted structures. Finally, we released GenomeScreenDB, an open-access database providing precomputed results for ~10,000 human proteins screened against 500 million compounds, pioneering a drug discovery paradigm in the post-AlphaFold era.

### Preprint abstract (bioRxiv, 2024-09-03)
Numerous protein-coding genes are associated with human diseases, yet approximately 90% of them lack targeted therapeutic intervention. While conventional computational methods such as molecular docking have facilitated the discovery of potential hit compounds, the development of genome-wide virtual screening against the expansive chemical space remains a formidable challenge. Here we introduce DrugCLIP, a novel framework that combines contrastive learning and dense retrieval to achieve rapid and accurate virtual screening. Compared to traditional docking methods, DrugCLIP improves the speed of virtual screening by several orders of magnitude. In terms of performance, DrugCLIP not only surpasses docking and other deep learning-based methods across two standard benchmark datasets but also demonstrates high efficacy in wet-lab experiments. Specifically, DrugCLIP successfully identified agonists with < 100 nM affinities for 5HT2AR, a key target in psychiatric diseases. For another target NET, whose structure is newly solved and not included in the training set, our method achieved a hit rate of 15%, with 12 diverse molecules exhibiting affinities better than Bupropion. Additionally, two chemically novel inhibitors were validated by structure determination with Cryo-EM. Building on this foundation, we present the results of a pioneering trillion-scale genome-wide virtual screening, encompassing approximately 10,000 AlphaFold2 predicted proteins within the human genome and 500 million molecules from the ZINC and Enamine REAL database. This work provides an innovative perspective on drug discovery in the post-AlphaFold era, where comprehensive targeting of all disease-related proteins is within reach.

### Problem (Introduction)
The human genome comprises ~20,000 protein-coding genes, but only about 10% have been successfully targeted by FDA-approved drugs or have documented small-molecule binders in the literature. Most researchers lack access to high-throughput screening equipment or sufficient compute for comprehensive virtual screening. Molecular docking remains time-intensive, often several seconds to minutes per protein–ligand pair: a recent large-scale campaign took two weeks to screen 1 billion molecules against a single target even with 10,000 CPU cores. Deep-learning affinity regression models face (i) inconsistency of affinity values across heterogeneous experimental conditions, (ii) distribution shift between curated training sets and real screens, which contain far larger proportions of inactive molecules, and (iii) inference-speed bottlenecks from million-parameter models.

### Method — DrugCLIP
DrugCLIP redefines virtual screening as a dense retrieval task. Pocket and molecule are encoded by separated neural networks into an aligned embedding space; vector similarity reflects binding probability. A contrastive loss maximizes similarity between a pocket and its binders (positive pairs) and minimizes it against molecules binding other targets (negatives).

Training has two stages. Pretraining: the molecule encoder is initialized with Uni-Mol and frozen; the randomly initialized pocket encoder is trained to align with it by contrastive distillation. Training data come from ProFSA (Protein Fragment-Surrounding Alignment): short peptide fragments extracted from protein-only structures serve as pseudo-ligands and their surrounding regions as pseudo-pockets, via fragment segmentation, terminal correction, neighbour removal and pocket detection. Applied to the PDB, ProFSA yielded 5.5 million pseudo pocket–ligand pairs. The pretrained pocket encoder was evaluated on pocket matching, pocket property prediction and protein–ligand affinity prediction, performing strongly even zero-shot, outperforming many supervised, physical and knowledge-based models.

Fine-tuning: both encoders are updated using 40,000 experimentally determined protein–ligand complex structures from BioLiP2. Because binding conformations are unknown in a real screen (only topology is given), random conformation sampling with RDKit is used for augmentation.

Screening: encoders map molecules and pockets to vectors; cosine similarities rank candidates. Molecule representations are computed offline, so screening needs only a cosine similarity and a sort — "more than 100,000 times faster" than docking. Complexity is O(M+N) in M targets and N compounds, where most existing methods are O(MN).

### In silico benchmarks
Benchmarks: DUD-E (22,886 active compounds over 102 protein targets; 50 property-matched, structurally different decoys per active) and LIT-PCBA (~8,000 actives and 2.64 million inactives over 15 targets, from PubChem BioAssay). Baselines: docking software Glide-SP, AutoDock Vina, Surflex; ML models NNScore, RFscore, Pafnucy, OnionNet, PLANET, Gnina, BigBind. DrugCLIP showed substantial improvement over all baselines in EF1% (enrichment factor at 1%, measuring recall).

Generalization: even with the test protein families entirely excluded from training, DrugCLIP still outperformed AutoDock Vina. Robustness: it outperformed Vina even with 3 Å RMSD error in pocket side-chain conformations.

Speed: DrugCLIP completed LIT-PCBA in 38 seconds with pre-encoding acceleration, versus Glide docking 3 days, Uni-Dock 22 hours, and the ML method PLANET 3 hours. Time scales linearly as target and molecule numbers increase together.

### Wet-lab validation (preprint): 5HT2AR
5HT2AR is an emerging antidepressant target; β-arrestin2 recruitment after activation is thought key. Because 5HT2AR structures were in the training data, a customized model was trained with similar experimental structures (90% homology) excluded to prevent leakage. A library of 1,648,137 in-stock ChemDiv molecules was screened and 78 top compounds ordered, selected on drug-likeness, docking score and chemical diversity.

A calcium flux assay at 10 μM in agonist mode gave 8 of 78 positives with at least 10% of serotonin's activity; [3H]-ketanserin competitive binding gave 6 with Ki < 10 μM; all six reached EC50 < 1 μM for β-arrestin2 recruitment in NanoBit assays. V008-4481, the highest-affinity molecule, binds the orthosteric, extended and side-extended pockets with its indole ring, phenylpiperazine part and linear tail respectively (molecules binding all three regions are rarely seen); affinity 21.0 nM, EC50 60.3 nM, Emax 35.8%. V006-3328 has a branched topology uncommon among 5HT2AR binders; affinity 3.51 μM, EC50 599 nM, Emax 23.4%.

### Wet-lab validation (preprint): NET
NET is a well-established target for depression and ADHD with multiple FDA-approved inhibitors, but structures with or without inhibitors were not solved until 2024. The closest structure in the training dataset is the Drosophila dopamine transporter, which shares less than 60% similarity with NET, so NET tests generalization to relatively new targets. The model trained on all collected complex structures screened the ChemDiv in-stock library; 104 compounds were selected for chemical novelty and diversity and tested for inhibition of [3H]-norepinephrine transport in NET-containing liposomes. 15% showed more than 60% inhibition of NET, and 12 compounds were more potent than bupropion. (Figure 3G legend: of 104 molecules assayed at 10 μM, 16 showed inhibition larger than 60%.)

Unlike previous NET inhibitors, which typically feature aliphatic nitrogen atoms forming a salt bridge with ASP75, the screen found hits with positively charged aromatic nitrogen atoms. 0086-0043 and Y510-9709 had IC50 1.14 μM and 0.31 μM respectively, better than bupropion (1.5 μM). Cryo-EM complex structures showed the isoquinoline ring of 0086-0043 in a T-shaped π–π interaction with PHE72, and the thiazole ring of Y510-9709 likely interacting with surrounding aromatic side chains such as PHE323.

### AlphaFold-structure screening and GenPack
Screening with AlphaFold-predicted structures is debated: predicted structures may lack the accuracy to replicate experimental conformations and filter inactives, though some studies report reasonable results for certain targets. Replacing DUD-E structures with AlphaFold predictions and using Fpocket to identify the nearest pockets dropped DrugCLIP's EF1% from 27.4% to 13.3%.

GenPack (Generation-Packing) trains molecular generative models conditioned on pocket backbone structures. Generated molecules, though not always synthesizable, help localize pockets more precisely and induce the pocket conformation into a more suitable state; side chains are then reintroduced and the conformation refined with physical force fields. GenPack raised DUD-E EF1% from 13.3% to 19.5% and the docking success rate (redocking RMSD < 2 Å) from 19.1% to 38.7%.

### Genome-wide screening pipeline and database
AlphaFold predictions were split into high-confidence regions by pLDDT and PAE scores. For each region, pockets were detected by homology alignment (TM-align), Fpocket, and GenPack. DrugCLIP then screened over 500 million drug-like molecules from ZINC and Enamine REAL. The process involved more than 10 trillion protein–ligand scoring operations and completed in about 24 hours on a single computing node with 8 A100 GPUs. Top-ranked molecules were clustered and evaluated by molecular docking, filtering out poor poses with Glide score < −6 kcal/mol. The final database contains over 2 million potential hit molecules for more than 20,000 pockets from 10,000 human targets, with docking scores and poses, freely accessible at https://drug-the-whole-genome.yanyanlan.com.

Coverage: UniProt contains 20,436 reviewed human proteins; ChEMBL 34 covers 4,810 of them, and not all those have high-affinity small-molecule binders (some only peptide or antibody binders, or vague low-quality assay results). This database spans 9,908 targets, more than twice ChEMBL, covering nearly half the human genome. Protein sequences were encoded with ESM1b and compared by t-SNE, showing a broader protein space than ChEMBL's.

Example targets: JNK3, a classical kinase with many ligand-bound crystal structures — identified molecules bind the ATP-binding pocket, forming H-bonds with backbone atoms of MET149 in the hinge region. SLC45A2, a solute-carrier family member with limited pharmacological study, important in pigmentation and widely expressed in cutaneous melanomas with suggested oncogenic potential — all database molecules bind near L374, a site important for protein stability. OR6A2, an olfactory receptor expressed in macrophages that senses blood octanal and promotes atherosclerotic plaque formation — predicted molecules fit its orthosteric pocket and could serve as potential inhibitors for treating atherosclerosis. Sestrin-2, a leucine sensor promoting cancer drug resistance, from a conserved stress-inducible family with only three human members — predicted molecules bind the same pocket as leucine, possible starting points for anti-cancer therapies.

### Conclusions and discussion (preprint)
DrugCLIP was validated on in silico benchmarks and in wet-lab experiments, consistently outperforming traditional docking software and contemporary ML models, and identifying diverse high-affinity binders and novel chemical entities for 5HT2AR and NET. The genome-wide campaign covered more than 20,000 pockets across ~10,000 human proteins with a 500-million-molecule library from ZINC and Enamine REAL, in 24 hours on one node with 8 GPU accelerators, producing over 2 million high-confidence protein–ligand complex structures with docking scores, released freely. The authors argue genome-wide screening enables screening all relevant targets simultaneously rather than a few, supports customized chemical libraries for phenotypic screening in high-fidelity models such as organoids or humanized mice, could scale to the largest ultra-large libraries available (e.g. the 48 billion-compound Enamine REAL Space), and could serve as a resource for retrieval-augmented molecular generation.
